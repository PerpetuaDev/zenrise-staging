Kamakura has preserved its temples for eight hundred and fifty years — and its stillness for just as long.

This tour is built around that stillness: not a rapid checklist of sights, but a unhurried journey through three of the city’s five great Zen temples, accompanied by a guide who brings their history to life and gives you ample time to truly absorb each moment.

The Five Mountains, or Kamakura Gozan, are the five preeminent Rinzai Zen temples whose foundations were laid under the Kamakura shogunate and which were later ranked at the highest tier by the Muromachi shogunate: Kencho‑ji, Engaku‑ji, Jufuku‑ji, Jochi‑ji, and Jomyo‑ji.

On this journey, you will experience a brief zazen meditation at Engaku-ji, stroll through the seasonal grounds of Jochi-ji, and savor matcha overlooking the dry landscape Zen garden at Jomyo-ji. The morning concludes with a relaxed lunch tailored to your taste.

Everything is arranged in advance. All you need to bring is a willingness to slow down. For full-day tours, the afternoon itinerary following lunch will be fully customized to your preferences.

The route.

01

Meet & Greet at Kita-Kamakura Station

The standard meeting point is Kita-Kamakura Station. Your guide will meet you outside the West Exit. We offer free pick-up and drop-off anywhere within Kamakura and Fujisawa cities. Please let us know your preferred location in advance. If you require hotel pick-up from Tokyo or other areas outside Kamakura/Fujisawa, or wish to arrange a private vehicle, we can provide a custom quote based on your departure point. Please feel free to inquire.

02

Engaku-ji — Zazen meditation—a stillness held in half-light.

Engaku‑ji ranks second among Kamakura’s Five Mountains, the great Zen temples of the old shogunate. Here, your guide leads a short zazen sitting — not a full formal session, but enough to feel, even briefly, what the practice asks of you: stillness, breath, and attention

03

Jochi-ji — Discovering Wabi-Sabi — The Quiet Beauty of Zen

Jochi‑ji, the fourth of the Five Mountains, keeps its grounds simply — a bamboo grove, a mossed stone stairway, flowers that change with the season. Here, there is little to do but walk slowly, let every sense settle into quiet, and take in the scenery as it unfolds.

04

Zen lunch (Shojin Ryori or Your Choice)

Traditional Shojin ryori (Zen Buddhist vegetarian cuisine) or a lunch tailored to your preferences, fully covered by the arrangement fee.

05

Jomyo-ji — A bowl of matcha, served in quiet.

Jomyo‑ji, ranked fifth among the Five Mountains, holds a beautiful Japanese tea house set quietly within its grounds, facing a dry landscape Zen garden raked into rippling lines. Here, matcha is served in quiet, the garden doing the talking.

06

Tour End & Drop-off

Complimentary drop-off within Kamakura and Fujisawa.
