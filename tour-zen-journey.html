Kamakura has kept its temples for eight hundred and fifty years, and its stillness for just as long.

This tour is built around that stillness — not a checklist of sights, but a slow pass through three of the city's five great Zen temples, with a guide who can explain what you're looking at, and enough quiet, unhurried time to actually look.

The Five Mountains, or Gozan, are the five preeminent Rinzai Zen temples whose foundations were laid under the Kamakura shogunate and which were later ranked at the highest tier by the Muromachi shogunate: Kencho-ji, Engaku-ji, Jufuku-ji, Jochi-ji, and Jomyo-ji.

You'll sit for a short zazen at Engaku-ji, wander the seasonal grounds of Jochi-ji, and take matcha before a dry rock garden at Jomyo-ji, before lunch closes the morning at a place suited to your taste.

Everything is arranged in advance. All you need to bring is a willingness to slow down.
