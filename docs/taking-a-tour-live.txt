TAKING A TOUR LIVE ON ZENRISE.JP
A step-by-step guide for the Zenrise team
Prepared 2026-08-27


HOW THIS WORKS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH
==============================================================================

The website reads your tours from Bokun. You do not edit the website. You edit
the tour in Bokun, add it to one product list, and within the hour the site
rebuilds itself: the tour page, the card on Our Tours, the tile on the home
page, the booking calendar, and the sitemap. There is no separate copy of your
tour text anywhere. Bokun is the only place it lives.

Practically, that means two things. Anything wrong in Bokun shows up on the
site within the hour — and anything you fix in Bokun is fixed on the site
within the hour, without asking anyone.


THE FOUR THINGS THAT DECIDE WHETHER A TOUR IS PUBLISHED
==============================================================================

A tour appears on the website only when ALL FOUR are true. If one is missing,
the tour is skipped quietly and the rest of the site carries on as normal.

  1. It is a Zenrise-tier tour, i.e. set to PRIVATE in Bokun.
     Public/OTA-styled tours are excluded, deliberately and doubly so.

  2. It is a member of the Bokun product list named 'Website'.
     This is the on/off switch. Nothing publishes without it.

  3. It has an English title.
     The page's web address is generated from it, once, the first time.

  4. It has a cover photo and a description.

A tour with no price is NOT skipped. It publishes with an 'in preparation'
layout instead of a booking calendar, which is intentional — it lets you put a
tour in front of people while you finish pricing it.


BEFORE YOU START — HAVE THESE READY
==============================================================================

  - Title, in English AND Japanese
  - Short description (one or two lines), both languages
  - Full description, both languages
  - A cover photo, and ideally one photo per itinerary step
  - What is included / not included / what to bring / good to know
  - Duration, group size, and at least one price
  - Which of these themes the tour belongs to:
        Temples & Shrines · Food · Walking · Views & Nature ·
        Local Life · Culture · Arts & Craft
  - Which area: Kamakura, Enoshima or Yokohama  (see CAUTION 1)


THE STEPS
==============================================================================

STEP 1 — Create the tour in Bokun and set it to PRIVATE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Bokun's visibility setting is what separates your own tours from the
  OTA-styled ones. PRIVATE tours are the ones the website considers.

  CAUTION: do not set it to PUBLIC to 'test' it. Public tours are excluded by
  two independent checks, so it will not appear either way, and PUBLIC also
  exposes it on the OTA channels.


STEP 2 — Fill in the title, in BOTH languages
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Set the Japanese title on the base version and the English title on the
  English version.

  CAUTION — THIS ONE IS PERMANENT: the page's web address is generated from
  the English title the FIRST time the tour publishes, and then frozen. If you
  publish 'The Zen Journey' the address becomes /tour-zen-journey.html and it
  stays that way even if you later rename the tour. That is deliberate — a
  changing address breaks every link anyone has shared.

  So: get the English title right BEFORE the tour goes into the Website list.
  If an address does come out wrong, tell your developer — it can be changed
  once, by hand.

  CAUTION: if the English title is missing, or is still the Japanese text, the
  tour will not publish at all. It is skipped until an English title exists.


STEP 3 — Write the short description and the full description
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Short description becomes the line under the tour name on the cards.
  Full description becomes the opening text on the tour page.

  The website respects your paragraphing. The FIRST paragraph is set large,
  as the opening statement; every paragraph after it is set smaller. So break
  your description into paragraphs where you want it broken.

  CAUTION: a line break placed in the MIDDLE of a sentence will split that
  sentence into two paragraphs on the site. If a paragraph ever looks oddly
  chopped, that is why — remove the stray break in Bokun.

  CAUTION: do not put a heading like 'Practical Notes' inside the description.
  It has no styling to become, so it reads as a stray phrase mid-paragraph.


STEP 4 — Tick the predefined inclusions and traveller information
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  These are Bokun's own tick-boxes (Bus fare, Parking fees, Entry &
  admission fees, Public transport nearby, and so on).

  They become the small chips shown directly under the description. They need
  no translation — the website supplies both languages itself.

  Tick only what is genuinely true. All twelve ticked reads as boilerplate.


STEP 5 — Write the four free-text blocks
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
      Included · Not included · What to bring · Good to know

  These become the 'Other info' section at the bottom of the page, in two
  columns. Each is optional; a block you leave empty simply does not appear.

  Write one item per line. Either bullets or plain paragraphs work — the site
  treats each bullet, or each paragraph, as one item.

  CAUTION: do not repeat your own heading inside the block. The site already
  prints 'INCLUDED', 'NOT INCLUDED' and so on, so a first line reading
  'What is included' appears twice.

  CAUTION — THE BIG ONE: do not put general booking conditions here.
  Cancellation charges, refunds, payment methods and insurance scope are all
  set out in full on the Booking Terms page, and the Other info section links
  to it. Repeating them here makes the block three times longer, and worse, a
  shortened version can CONTRADICT the terms page. Keep this section to what
  is specific to THIS tour.


STEP 6 — Build the itinerary, and give each step a photo
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Each itinerary step becomes a numbered row in 'The route' section.

  Assign a KEY PHOTO to each step. It becomes the small thumbnail beside that
  row, with the step number set on the image. A step with no photo still works
  — it shows the number on its own — but the section looks much better with
  them, and mixing the two looks unintentional. Do all of them or none.

  Keep the step title short. It is set large, and a long one wraps awkwardly.

  CAUTION: the itinerary needs its own Japanese. It is a separate translation
  from the description, and if you leave it, a Japanese visitor reads English
  in that section while the rest of the page is Japanese.


STEP 7 — Upload photos
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Upload the LARGEST version you have. Do not resize them first.

  The website requests its own sizes from Bokun's image service — a wide one
  for the page banner, a smaller one for the cards, a small one for the route
  thumbnails. Uploading a pre-shrunk photo only limits what it can use.

  The first photo becomes the page banner and the card image, so put your
  strongest image first.


STEP 8 — Set the duration, group size, rates and prices
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CAUTION — RATE NAMES MATTER MORE THAN THEY LOOK. The site works out whether
  a tour is a half day, a full day, or both by READING YOUR RATE NAMES. Bokun
  does not record a duration per rate, so the names are the only signal.

  Keep the words 'Half Day' and 'Full Day' in the ENGLISH rate names:
        Group (1-6) Half Day        <- good
        Group (1-6) Full Day        <- good
        Group (1-6) Morning         <- the site cannot tell, and will fall
                                       back to the overall duration
  Japanese rate names can be written freely; 半日 and 一日 are both understood.

  A tour with both a half-day and a full-day rate shows 'Full / Half-day' in
  the FORMAT column. A tour with neither word falls back to the overall
  duration, and a four-hour tour that also sells a full day would then be
  labelled 'Half-day' — understating what you offer.

  CAUTION: the price shown on the cards is the LOWEST rate, shown as 'from'.
  A tour with a 40,000 half day and a 70,000 full day advertises 'from
  40,000'. That is normal practice, but be aware the higher rate is only
  visible inside the booking calendar.


STEP 9 — Optional extras
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CAUTION: give every extra a PRICE. An extra with no price set will show as
  free, or be refused at checkout. Either is worse than not offering it.

  CAUTION: name extras by what they buy, not by number. 'Arrangement Fee —
  #2' tells a guest nothing about why it costs twice '#1'. Name them for the
  thing itself, and translate the names — extras are shown inside the booking
  calendar, where an untranslated name is the last thing a Japanese guest sees
  before paying.


STEP 10 — Add the tour to the 'Website' product list
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THIS IS THE PUBLISH BUTTON. Nothing before this step puts anything on the
  site, so you can take as long as you like getting steps 1-9 right.

  Removing a tour from the list unpublishes it: its page and its short link
  are deleted on the next rebuild. The tour itself stays in Bokun untouched,
  so availability and existing bookings are unaffected.

  CAUTION: an EMPTY Website list means 'publish nothing', and is obeyed. It is
  not treated as a mistake. Do not empty the list to test something.


STEP 11 — Wait up to an hour, then check the site
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The site rebuilds every hour, on the hour. There is nothing to trigger.

  If you need it immediately, your developer can run it in under a minute.


CHECK THIS AFTER IT GOES LIVE
==============================================================================

  [ ] The tour appears on Our Tours, with the right photo and price
  [ ] It appears under the right theme when you use the theme filters
  [ ] The tour page opens, and the address looks sensible
  [ ] FORMAT reads correctly (Half-day / Full-day / Full / Half-day)
  [ ] The route section shows your thumbnails and step numbers
  [ ] The booking calendar loads and offers real dates
  [ ] Switch the site to Japanese, top right, and re-read the WHOLE page:
        title, description, itinerary, Other info, and the calendar itself
  [ ] Other info is not repeating the Booking Terms page

  On the language switch: the page reloads when you change language on a tour
  page. That is intentional. The booking calendar can only pick up its
  language when it first loads, so the page reloads to give it a clean start.


CAUTIONS, COLLECTED
==============================================================================

CAUTION 1 — AREA MUST BE ONE WE ALREADY HAVE
  The site knows three areas: Kamakura, Enoshima, Yokohama. A tour whose city
  is anything else is held back with a warning until a developer adds the new
  area, because the area needs a Japanese translation and a filter button.
  The rest of the site keeps working normally. If you are planning a tour in a
  new city, tell your developer BEFORE you add it to the Website list.

CAUTION 2 — THEMES STILL NEED A DEVELOPER
  Themes are the only part of a tour a developer still sets. A tour with no
  theme publishes perfectly well; it simply will not appear under any theme
  filter. Send the theme along when you add the tour and it takes a minute.

CAUTION 3 — JAPANESE IS HELD BACK UNTIL IT IS REVIEWED
  Japanese text does not go live automatically. Each tour has a review flag,
  set by your developer, and until it is set the site shows English in both
  languages rather than risk publishing raw machine translation. Tell your
  developer when a tour's Japanese is finished and checked.

CAUTION 4 — MACHINE TRANSLATION IS NOT A DRAFT
  Bokun can machine-translate a tour in one click. The output has reached the
  site before and was not usable: mangled words, split characters, sentences
  in the wrong order. If you use it, treat it as a starting point to be
  rewritten, and do not ask for the review flag until a person has read it.

CAUTION 5 — SPACING DAMAGE FROM PASTED TEXT
  Text pasted from a PDF or a design file has arrived with words broken
  mid-character — 'templ e cuisine', 'passag e through'. The site used to
  carry a repair list for these; it is now empty because they were all fixed
  at source. Please keep it that way: paste into a plain text editor first,
  then into Bokun.

CAUTION 6 — THE SHORT LINK
  Every published tour also gets a short link for social posts. It is created
  and removed automatically with the tour. One older link, used in an
  Instagram post, points at an OTA tour and is maintained separately — do not
  be surprised that it does not match a Website-list tour.

CAUTION 7 — THE BOOKING CALENDAR'S APPEARANCE
  The calendar is Bokun's, shown inside the page, so it cannot use the site's
  typefaces — those are licensed to zenrise.jp and will not load inside
  Bokun's frame. It has been styled to match the site's colours as closely as
  possible. It is also fixed at around 420px wide by Bokun and cannot be made
  wider, which is why the booking column is the width it is.


IF THE TOUR HAS NOT APPEARED
==============================================================================

Work down this list — it is in the order the site checks things.

  1. Is it in the 'Website' product list?              -> most common cause
  2. Is it set to PRIVATE, not PUBLIC?
  3. Does it have an ENGLISH title, distinct from the Japanese one?
  4. Does it have a cover photo?
  5. Does it have a description?
  6. Is its city one of Kamakura / Enoshima / Yokohama?
  7. Has an hour passed since the change?

If all seven are yes, ask your developer to check the build log. Every skipped
tour is named there with the exact reason, so it is a quick answer rather than
an investigation.

A tour that has appeared but shows 'in preparation' instead of a calendar is
not broken: it has no price yet.


WHAT A DEVELOPER STILL DOES
==============================================================================

  - Sets a tour's theme (Caution 2)
  - Sets the Japanese review flag (Caution 3)
  - Adds a new area (Caution 1)
  - Corrects a web address if one comes out badly (Step 2)
  - Adds new wording to the Booking Terms page

Everything else — text, photos, itinerary, prices, extras, publishing and
unpublishing — is yours, and takes effect within the hour.
